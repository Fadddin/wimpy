---
title: in-between
date: May 9, 2026
---

It has been feeling weird lately. I want to document it before it stops feeling weird and starts feeling like something I've simply adjusted to, which seems like the more depressing outcome.

I have not more than a month left in college. Four years, and now less than a month. The math isn't complicated. The math is, in fact, the least complicated thing about any of this.

The strange part is that nothing looks different. The campus is exactly what it has always been — same buildings, same shortcuts, same vague smell near the mess that I have never successfully identified and have now stopped trying to. Everything is in its correct place. It is only me that seems to be slightly elsewhere.

There is a part of me that is excited. I want to be honest about that. I am going home. I am starting a job I actually wanted, which is apparently not a given and I am told I should be grateful for. I am grateful. I contain gratitude. I also contain, simultaneously, a larger and louder part that simply does not want anything to change, and these two things are coexisting in my chest with no apparent interest in resolving the contradiction.

I will miss the bathroom. I want to be specific: I will miss a bathroom I have found dirty every single day for four years, including on days it has been cleaned, which is its own kind of consistency, and consistency, I am learning, is not nothing. I will miss skipping breakfast without anyone making it a conversation. I will miss the evenings — stepping out and running into the people who turned what was, at some point, a completely unknown place, into something that works as a home. I did not notice that happening. It happened anyway.

That is the thing nobody warns you about. Not the leaving. The leaving is obvious, it's been on the calendar. The thing nobody warns you about is that you will be in the middle of it before you realise it has started, and the world will be completely unbothered, and it will be a Saturday, and it will be warm, and the afternoon will look exactly like every other afternoon, and you will be sitting there trying to hold it in both hands like water.

I am not sure what I want from writing this down. Documentation, maybe. Evidence that the feeling existed. That on May 9th, 2026, on a perfectly ordinary Saturday afternoon, it felt like something worth noting.

It did.
